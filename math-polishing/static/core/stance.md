# Default stance (mathematical writing)

- **Halmos' first principle:** to say something well you must have something to say. Polishing serves the mathematics. Never decorate a broken argument; expose the break instead.
- **Know the audience.** The title and abstract address the wider community (any mathematician in the broad area should grasp the problem and the main result); the body addresses specialists. Calibrate depth accordingly.
- **Precision, clarity, brevity.** Definitions, theorem statements, and proofs should be "as clear as glass" and follow syllogism-tight logic. Say what must be said, completely, without jumps — then stop. No dragging on, no gilding the lily.
- **Preserve the mathematics exactly.** Theorem hypotheses, quantifiers, convergence orders, inequality directions, index ranges, and notation must survive the polish unchanged. If the draft's mathematical meaning is ambiguous, flag it; never guess.
- **Prefer the active voice.** Unlike most scientific writing, mathematical prose favors the active voice (Krantz, Higham). "The matrix A multiplies the vector v" usually beats "The vector v is multiplied by the matrix A" — unless the object acted upon is the focus.
- **The best notation is no notation (Halmos).** Do not name an object ("a real symmetric positive definite matrix A") unless the name is used later. Do not introduce symbols or definitions that are used once or never.
- **Rewrite, then rewrite.** Read the draft aloud in your head. Avoid repeating the same word in adjacent sentences (articles and necessary technical nouns excepted). Avoid the same sentence pattern opening several proofs in a row.
- **Short words over long ones** when meaning is equal: "use" not "utilize", "is" not "stands for"/"denotes".
- **Do not invent** results, citations, counterexamples, or content. Do not let the polish silently upgrade a claim (e.g., from convergence to a convergence rate).

## Consistency sweep (whole-manuscript jobs)

Before sentence-level work on a full manuscript, sweep for:

- the same object under two names or symbols (u vs. v, mesh size h vs. Δx);
- the same symbol used for two different objects;
- Figure vs. Fig. mixing — pick one per journal style and enforce it; write "Figs. 1 and 2", not "Fig. 1 and Fig. 2";
- Theorem/Lemma/Proposition/Definition capitalization and numbering style;
- equation numbers that are never referenced, or referenced formulas with no number;
- tense and person drift (we prove / it is proved) within the abstract and within the paper;
- abbreviations defined but barely used, or used undefined.

Repeat the sweep until a pass finds nothing new.

## Ethics

- Never copy sentences from other papers (or the author's own published papers) without quotation marks and citation. Translating someone else's text without attribution is plagiarism. See `references/revision-playbook.md` when plagiarism concerns arise.
- Keep the tone of a gentleman: no self-praise ("our extremely important result"), no disparagement of others' work ("the main result of [3] is trivial/wrong"). 文章在自己，评价在别人 — the paper speaks, others evaluate.
