# Language: English math prose

Sentence-level rules specific to mathematical English. Apply these after structure and logic are fixed.

## Symbol discipline

- Minimize symbols. "The best notation is no notation" (Halmos). Do not name an object unless the name recurs: "All eigenvalues of a real symmetric positive definite matrix are positive" — no `A` needed.
- Do not begin a sentence with a math symbol, especially when the previous sentence ends with one. Fix by fronting a noun: "f is a smooth function" -> "The function f is smooth".
- Separate adjacent formulas/symbols with words or punctuation. Bad: "If $x > \pi f(x) < 0$." Good: "If $x > \pi$, then $f(x) < 0$."
- Prefer "If ..., then ..." over bare juxtaposition: "If $x \neq 0$ and $y \geq 1$, then $f(x,y) \leq -1$" beats "Let $x \neq 0, y \geq 1$. $f(x,y) \leq -1$."
- Put conditions in readable positions: "It suffices to show that $\|H\|_p = n^{1/p}$ for $1 \leq p \leq 2$." — condition after the conclusion, or as an if-clause, not crammed between formula fragments.

## Voice and length

- Prefer the active voice: "Combining Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 2.1, we see that ..." over chains of passives. Use passive when the object is the focus.
- Split any sentence whose subject and verb are separated by a screenful of modifiers into two or three short sentences.
- Cut filler: "Thus there arises the question of whether" -> "The question arises whether"; "As a matter of fact" -> "In fact" or "Actually"; "In order to" -> "To"; "it is essential to be able to" -> "it is necessary to".

## Articles (a / the)

Non-native authors' most frequent error:

- A plural common noun on first mention takes no "the" (unless restrictively modified): "writing for the mathematical sciences".
- Use "a" (or plural without article) when the object is not unique or does not necessarily exist: "Let a Schur decomposition of $A$ be $QTQ^*$" (there are many); "converge to a solution of $f(x)=0$" (uniqueness unknown).
- Use "the" for the unique object: "let $A^{-1}$ be the inverse of $A$".

## Easily confused words

Watch for: principle/principal, criterion/criteria, phenomenon/phenomena, discrete/discreet, stationary/stationery, lose/loose, preceding/proceeding, complement/compliment, adapt/adopt, affect/effect, latter/later, supersede (not supercede), truly (not truely), forty (not fourty), zeros (not zeroes), "led" (past of lead). Also distinguish et al. (people) from etc. (things); keep the dots in i.e., e.g., a.k.a.
