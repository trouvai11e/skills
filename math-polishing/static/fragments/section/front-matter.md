# Section: Front matter (byline, date, keywords, subject classification)

Everything between the title and the first section: author names, affiliations, date, keywords, and the Mathematics Subject Classification line.

## Byline (署名)

- **Order.** Pure and applied mathematics convention is alphabetical by family name — order does not signal contribution. (Contribution-based ordering is the engineering/experimental convention.) Note the asymmetry readers apply: if order is not alphabetical, they read the first-listed author as the minor contributor. Citations often surface only the first author ("Liu et al. (1990)"), and some indexes search only first authors.
- **Name form — fix it early and keep it for life.** Write the given name first: "Weigang Zhang" or "Wei-Gang Zhang", not "Zhang Weigang" (risks being indexed as family name "Weigang"). Prefer the hyphenated initial form "W.-G. Zhang" over "W. Zhang" — fewer name collisions, easier retrieval. Do not add a later-adopted Western first name to paper bylines; indexes will treat you as two people.
- After the names, give every author's affiliation, postal address, and email.
- **Ethics.** Do not add or remove authors after review without explaining to the editor and getting permission. Never list anyone (e.g., the advisor) without their consent — a byline signs responsibility, not honor.

## Date

- Put the date of completion on the line after the author names; date each revision too. It tracks the writing process and lets others cite an unpublished draft with its time and version.
- Citing unpublished work: "(... 2001, unpublished)" or "(... 2001, private communication)".

## Keywords

- Usually at most 10 (long, concept-rich papers may use more). Keywords feed Mathematical Reviews/SCI archiving and reader search — missing keywords mean missed retrieval, fewer citations, less impact. Do not choose them carelessly.
- Negative list: over-general words (computation, fluid mechanics, mathematics) and evaluative adjectives (new, interesting, best) carry no retrieval value.
- Positive strategy: one or two broad-area words plus several precise narrow ones — e.g., "numerical approximation, finite element method, mesh adaptation, local time stepping".
- Form: phrases are allowed; prefer singular nouns ("finite element method", not "... methods"). Beware pseudo-plurals: "statistics" and "chaos" are not plurals.

## Subject classification (MSC)

- The Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC2010 and later), maintained by Mathematical Reviews and Zentralblatt MATH, has 97 top-level areas with tree-like subdivisions. It places the paper correctly in the archive and lets its potential readers find it — advertising for the paper. (Adjacent systems: Computing Reviews classification for computing; PACS for physics.)
- Journal format is a fixed template:

  `2000 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 13B25, 14J70, 14R10.`
  `Key words and phrases. Coordinates, locally nilpotent derivations, embeddings.`

- Multiple codes are comma-separated after "Primary" (a long paper may list many); keywords follow on the next line, lowercase except proper nouns, ending with a period.
