# Section: Acknowledgments

Short and simple. "Acknowledgments" (US) / "Acknowledgements" (UK) — follow the journal.

## Whom to thank

- Funding agencies, with grant/contract numbers where required — many grants mandate acknowledgment:
  "This work was supported in part by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12345678)."
- People who gave useful comments, discussions, hospitality, or corrections: "We are grateful to Prof. X for many useful discussions."
- Anonymous referees only when they made a substantial contribution (a shorter proof, a thorough language overhaul). Routine refereeing needs no thanks.
  "We are grateful to the two anonymous referees for useful comments and suggestions." — "anonymous", never "unknown".

## Wording discipline

- Prefer the shortest form: "I thank" over "I would like to thank".
- Standard patterns: "The financial support of ... is gratefully acknowledged." / "The author is indebted to Dr. J. Smith for many helpful discussions, and to Dr. S. Fisher for correcting two errors in an earlier version." — note the repeated "to" before the second person.
- Long thesis-style acknowledgments (supervisor, committee, family) belong in theses, not journal papers.
