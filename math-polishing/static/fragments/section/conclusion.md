# Section: Conclusions (Concluding Remarks)

Overlap with abstract and introduction should be slight; most content must be new.

## What goes in

1. A very short summary of the main contribution — fresh wording, not recycled abstract/introduction sentences; restate the importance of the results.
2. Return to the questions raised in the introduction and say what can now be answered.
3. Aspects or broader problems the paper did not treat (e.g., for reasons of length), and whether the methods extend to them.
4. Implications of the research and its use for related problems.
5. The next step / future work; related conjectures if appropriate.

## What stays out

- No apologies or false modesty ("the poor quality of this paper").
- No long lists of new difficult questions.
- No brand-new ideas or material.
- No change of tone or style from the rest of the paper.
- Nothing contradicting any earlier part of the paper.
- No exaggerated claims.
- No verbatim restatement of the introduction.
- Careful with promising a follow-up paper ("will be published elsewhere") — only with real certainty; empty promises age badly.

## Wording

Standard closing connectors: in summary, to summarize, in conclusion, to conclude, finally, in brief — use one or two, not a rotation of all. A fuller phrase list is in `references/math-phrasebook.md`.
