# Section: Appendix

Appendices are optional in research papers; short pure-math papers usually have none (statement and proof are read together). An appendix earns its place in two situations:

1. **Long or highly technical proofs** that would clog the body — serves two reader groups: those who want every derivation step and those (especially in applied fields) who only need the results. Keep a proof sketch near the theorem statement when the paper is aimed partly at applied readers; pure-math papers normally keep proof and statement together.
2. **Prerequisites the proofs need** — standard lemmas, definitions, background results. Functionally this is a "Preliminaries" section placed at the end; most readers will read it, so treat it as part of the paper, not as a dumping ground.

## Rules

- **Write the appendix as well as the body.** It must not be slighted because it sits behind the references — same precision, same notation discipline, same proof standards.
- Use it for material that is inconvenient in the main text but should not be thrown away.
- Number appendix sections and equations distinctly (Appendix A, (A.1), ...) and reference them from the body where the reader needs the pointer.
- If the outline paragraph in the introduction mentions sections, mention the appendix there too when it carries a major proof.
