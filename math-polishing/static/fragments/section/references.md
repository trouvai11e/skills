# Section: References

## Content rules

- Every listed entry must be cited in the text; every citation must appear in the list. Some publishers (AMS, SIAM) check this one-to-one.
- Include the field's key papers and cite them in the introduction — the list demonstrates command of the literature. Do not pad with irrelevant entries; do not omit important ones; prefer sources readers can actually obtain.
- Cite primary sources. If the original is unavailable, at least read its abstract and two papers citing it before citing it indirectly.
- Math journals order references alphabetically by first author's family name (then second author, then year). Physics-style citation-order numbering is wrong for most math journals.
- Math reference entries include the article title — unlike physics/chemistry practice.
- Complete fields: authors, article title, journal (standard abbreviation — AMS MathSciNet maintains the canonical list, e.g., J. Math. Anal. Appl., SIAM J. Sci. Comput.), volume(issue), year, page range. Books: author, title, publisher, edition, city, year. Proceedings: also editors. Theses: the university.
- Journal names can change over time (SIAM J. Sci. Stat. Comput. before 1993 vs. SIAM J. Sci. Comput. after); use the name at the publication year.
- When citing a specific result from a book or long paper, add the locator: "by Banach's lemma ([7]; Theorem 4.5.3)".
- Unpublished work is labeled with time and status: "(... 2001, unpublished)" or "(... 2001, private communication)".

## Citation style in text

- Numbered (common in math): "the stability problem was first considered in [7]". Multiple citations in one bracket, ascending: "[3, 5, 9]", not "[3], [5], [9]".
- Harvard (author–year): "first considered by Bellman (1973)"; same author same year: (1973a), (1973b).
- Place the citation where it reads fluently: "This bound was found [10] to be sharp" beats "This bound was found to be sharp [10]". Naming the author helps: "found by Courant [10] to be sharp"; adding the year merges both styles: "Scarf (1967) [14]".
- Two authors: both names. Three or more: all names at first citation, then "First-author et al." Write "et al." exactly — not "et al" or "et. al." Distinguish et al. (people) from etc. (things). Keep the dots in i.e. and e.g.
- When a cited result is the starting point of the paper, name the authors and summarize their contribution and what they left open — a bare number is not enough.

## Consistency

- One format for all entries, matched to the target journal: author name order, journal abbreviation vs. full name, "and" vs. "&", "pp." usage, year placement. Do not paste entries from different journals' pages and stack them unedited — reformat all to the target style.
- Keep a personal .bib file with full records; format at submission time.
